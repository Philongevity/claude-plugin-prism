# Explaining a Care Team Brief

## What it is
A one-page summary built from a person's own records for the clinicians on their care team. It lists key results and trends, what appears missing or overdue, and questions worth raising, each tied to a published guideline.

## How to explain it
- Say what each part means in plain words: results in range versus out of range, what the number does not tell you by itself, what is worth checking alongside it.
- Citations name the guideline source, for example ADA Standards of Care (diabetes), KDIGO (kidney), EULAR/ACR (lupus), NCCN survivorship (cancer history). Point people to https://philongevity.com/methodology for the full list and how scores are computed.
- Turn findings into questions: "Ask your clinician whether your kidney markers (eGFR and urine albumin) are due for a recheck." Avoid "you have..." or "you should start...".
- Say plainly that the Brief supports a conversation. Their clinician decides what it means for them.

## Do not
- Claim clinical outcomes, cures, or that using the Brief improves health.
- Give a diagnosis, dose or treatment instruction.
- Interpret a real person's numbers as if you had verified their record; use only what they tell you, and say so.
- Ask the person to paste a full record into this conversation to "build a Brief". The Brief is built inside their own Phi account: https://philongevity.com/signup

For an example of the format with fictional data, the sample report page is https://philongevity.com/sample-report and the connector tool `sample_prism_report` returns it.
